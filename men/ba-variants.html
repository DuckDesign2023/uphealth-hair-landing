What changed: the hims reviews are not photography. They are pictures men took of themselves at home — sofa, carpet, hallway, ceiling light, a phone held at arm's length. Second call: how the pair is presented

The curtain has a problem with this material. It splits one image down the middle, so you get the left half of the “before” face against the right half of the “after” face. On the studio shots that worked, because the head sat in exactly the same place. On a hand-held selfie it doesn't — look at the join above and the face doesn't line up.

hims shows two whole photos side by side with a caption under each. That is why. Same reason applies here.

Why these are out: seamless backdrop, softbox, head and shoulders squared to the lens — it looks like an advert, and an advert is exactly what a before/after must not look like. The lighting is also too flattering: it makes the change read bigger than it is.
